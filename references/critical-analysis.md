# Critical Analysis Protocol

Separate author claims from independent analysis.

## Four analysis layers

### 1. Assumptions
What must be true for the method to work? Examples include reliable uncertainty estimates, stable feature spaces, representative validation data, or meaningful pseudo-labels. Use only assumptions relevant to the paper.

### 2. Evidence strength
For each central claim, identify the evidence:
- theorem/proof;
- controlled experiment;
- ablation;
- benchmark comparison;
- qualitative visualization;
- author discussion only.

Do not treat a visualization as quantitative proof.

### 3. Limitations and failure modes
Distinguish:
- limitations explicitly acknowledged by authors;
- limitations implied by the experimental design;
- plausible failure modes inferred by the explainer.

Label each category clearly.

### 4. Research implications
Offer concrete, bounded ideas:
- what component could be tested under a new condition;
- what assumption could be relaxed;
- what comparison is missing;
- what failure mode deserves evaluation.

Do not present speculative ideas as established research gaps. Explain why each idea follows from the paper.
